— The Sacred Story

The Shrine Eternal

Situated at Prabhas Patan on the western coast of Gujarat, the Somnath Temple is believed to be the first among the twelve Jyotirlinga shrines of Shiva. It is a highly revered pilgrimage site that has stood as a symbol of India's eternal spirit.

According to ancient texts, the Moon God (Soma) was cursed by his father-in-law Daksha to lose his luster. He bathed in the Sarasvati River at this very site to regain it. In reverence, Soma built the original temple in gold. It was subsequently built in silver by Ravana, in wood by Krishna, and in stone by Bhimdev.

The temple has withstood the ravages of time and history. It was destroyed and looted at least seventeen times by various invaders, most notably by Mahmud of Ghazni in 1024 CE. Yet, each time it fell, the devotion of the people resurrected it, making it the ultimate symbol of resilience. The present magnificent structure was rebuilt post-independence under the vision of Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel.

— Architectural Marvel

Kailash Mahameru Prasad

Chaulukya Style

The present temple is built in the Chalukya (Solanki) style of temple architecture, reflecting the skill of the Sompuras, Gujarat's master masons. It features exquisite carvings, a majestic Sabha Mandap, and a towering Shikhara.

The Baan Stambh

The Arrow Pillar stands on the sea-protection wall. It bears an ancient inscription stating that it stands at a point on the Indian landmass intersecting a straight line spanning the oceans all the way to Antarctica, with no land in between.

Triveni Sangam

Located near the confluence of three holy rivers: Kapila, Hiran, and the mythical Sarasvati. The coastal geography adds immense spiritual weight to the temple's profound aura.
